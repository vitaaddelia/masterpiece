01 · THE PROBLEM

Doctors couldn't trace their own salary.

On payday, doctors saw a single number with no context and no breakdown. Deductions had no labels, tax calculations weren't shown, and any question meant emailing HR and waiting up to three days.

No audit trail

Doctors had no way to trace why their payout didn't match expectations. Deductions appeared without a label or a reason.

Slow answers from HR

One salary question meant emailing HR, waiting three days, and often escalating.

Data in three places

Income, consultation fees and tax records each lived in a different system.

Low adoption

The old finance module was hard to use, so doctors called HR instead.

02 · THE NORTH STARS

Three rules. No exceptions.

Before opening Figma, we agreed on three principles that every screen had to follow.

Show the summary first

A doctor has 10 mins between patients. They need net pay, key deductions, anything weird, and nothing else. No data dumps.

Every number is traceable

If something looks off, they can tap it and get the full story. What it is, why it's there, how it was calculated. Full transparency.

No dead ends

Comparing months, disputing deductions, drilling into data: none of it should feel like a maze. Every path forward has a clear way back.

03 · THE MOVES

Four design decisions that mattered most.

Move 01 · Dashboard: "Your eye already knows where to look."

Profile and income land on the left, because that's where your eye goes first (following F- and Z-pattern scanning). The answer to "how much did I earn this month" hits in under 2 seconds. No scrolling. No hunting.

Anchoring key info to reading patterns cut time-to-comprehension by ~40% in testing.

Move 02 · Dynamic chart: "Wait, why is this less than expected?" answered.

The number one question in every interview: "Am I actually getting what I was promised?" We built a donut chart that shows expected vs. actual, with a hover state that surfaces each variable (tax, BPJS, fees, allowances) without turning the screen into a spreadsheet.

That hover interaction made doctors 3× more likely to actually explore their deductions.

Move 03 · Detail view: "Show me which patient, not just the total."

Doctors kept asking: "which consultation is contributing what?" We built a detail page one tap from the dashboard that breaks income down by patient, consultation type, and service code. Toggle between macro view and patient-level in seconds.

This removed the most common salary question doctors sent to HR.

Move 04 · Keep on flow: "You always know where you are. And how to go back."

Disputing a deduction is already stressful. Getting lost mid-flow makes it worse. So every multi-step journey has a step tracker, a running summary of what you've done, and the freedom to go back at any point without losing your progress.

Drop-off in multi-step flows went from 47% to 11% after adding those step summaries.
05 · ON MOBILE

Same three rules, on a 6-inch screen.

Doctors check pay between patients, often on their phone. So I took the same north stars to mobile. Hover doesn't exist on touch, so every hover state became a tap-to-expand row, which also makes the detail reachable by keyboard and screen readers.

06 · DESIGN SYSTEM

Built once, reused on every screen.

Every screen in this case study comes from one component library in Figma, built with Variables and Auto Layout. Colours are named by what they mean, not what they look like, so engineers and designers use the same words.
