OVERVIEW

How do we win conversion in a market we'd never designed for before?

DSCommerce was expanding into the DACH region (Germany, Austria, Switzerland), and needed landing pages and storefronts that converted with local shoppers — not just a translated version of what worked elsewhere.

Local Research

Understanding how DACH shoppers browse, compare, and decide differently from other markets.

Rapid Landing Pages

Shipping and testing dozens of page variants quickly, rather than perfecting one.

Iterating on Data

Using heatmaps and session recordings to find friction and prioritize the next test.

PROBLEM

Generic pages don't convert in every market.

DSCommerce's existing pages were built for a broad, English-speaking audience. In the DACH market, shoppers expected more explicit trust signals, clearer shipping and return terms up front, and a more formal tone — none of which the existing templates accounted for.

"WHAT WASN'T WORKING"
No trust signals above the fold
Shipping terms buried in checkout
Generic, translated copy

This was costing DSCommerce both traffic and trust: visitors were bouncing before they ever reached a product page, and the ones who did convert were doing so at a much lower rate than in established markets.

SOLUTION

Landing pages built around what DACH shoppers actually needed to see first.

A modular landing page system: trust signals and shipping terms surfaced immediately, localized (not just translated) copy, and a testing rhythm that let the team ship a new variant every week.

CORE FLOWS

From ad click to checkout, redesigned end to end

Trust signals, above the fold

Shipping terms, return policy, and local payment methods are visible before a shopper has to scroll.

Localized product copy

Copy rewritten for tone and local expectations, not machine-translated from the English original.

One-click test variants

A component system that let the team assemble a new page variant in under an hour.

RESEARCH

Understanding how DACH shoppers actually browse

Before redesigning anything, the team reviewed session recordings and heatmaps from existing pages, and benchmarked against local competitors to understand what shoppers expected to see.

DESIGN DECISIONS

Optimizing for trust before persuasion

Early variants leaned on urgency and discounting, which tested poorly. The pages that won were the ones that answered practical questions first — shipping cost, return window, payment options — before asking for a purchase decision.

Shoppers here don't need to be convinced to buy — they need to be convinced it's safe to.

That shift in framing became the basis for every page built afterward, and is what ultimately drove the jump in conversion.

REFLECTION

What I learned

Localization is more than translation.

The biggest wins came from rethinking what a page needed to say, not just how it said it.

Speed of testing compounds.

Shipping a variant every week mattered more than any single "perfect" redesign.
